# REVO — Product and Challenge Brief

## 1. Competition context

**Competition:** NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 — Cairo  
**Challenge:** Build a Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer  
**Target audience:** children aged 7–12  
**Chosen setting:** a fictional Mars outpost near Jezero Crater  
**Format:** browser-playable 2D game  

The challenge asks participants to design an interactive game or app that lets children manage a lunar or Martian outpost and make decisions involving life support, radiation shielding, power budgeting, food production, and communication delay. Players should experience how decisions play out over a simulated mission.

The team's supplied challenge brief emphasizes a gap between abstract space education and the real decision-making logic behind missions. It encourages mechanics—not only visuals—that mirror genuine constraints, authentic NASA open data where possible, clear attribution, low barriers to entry, and a working prototype that teaches a real trade-off.

## 2. Problem statement

Space education for children can be too abstract to show the trade-offs astronauts and mission planners face. Purely educational material may struggle to hold attention; entertainment without scientific grounding can lose accuracy. Children need a safe, accessible way to explore why a mission has competing needs and why no decision is completely free.

## 3. REVO's proposed solution

REVO is a short 2D top-down exploration and mission-management game. The player controls REVO, a young astronaut, and receives guidance from N.E.I.L. in the central Communication Room. N.E.I.L. is the friendly physical robot companion Navigation & Exploration Intelligence Link; its hybrid Mission Intelligence System role uses authored, deterministic rules against authoritative mission state to provide alerts, guide decisions, and explain outcomes. Terminals are interfaces, not N.E.I.L.'s identity.

The player explores Alpha Base, inspects facilities, discovers operational problems, chooses among limited options, and sees consequences across the base. After a choice, N.E.I.L. gives a brief explanation and, where relevant, a science card referencing NASA research or mission data.

## 4. The central learning mechanic

**Discover → Decide → See the Consequence → Understand → Try Again.**

Example: a limited power budget means the player cannot maximize every facility at once. Prioritizing life support may reduce research operations; prioritizing research may delay another useful task. The player sees the effect, learns why the trade-off happened, and can use that learning later.

Do not create a safe choice with no cost in every scenario. Trade-offs should be understandable and fair, not arbitrary or punitive.

## 5. Why Mars and why Jezero

REVO uses a fictional research outpost near Jezero Crater. Perseverance landed in Jezero and carries MEDA, which measures environmental conditions such as wind, temperature, humidity, and dust. This provides a real Mars science context for selected educational scenarios.

The outpost itself, crew, buildings, systems, mission schedule, and events are fictional. Do not imply NASA operates a human settlement at Jezero.

## 6. Intended impact

A child should finish the experience understanding that:

- Space exploration depends on systems working together.
- Resources are limited and must be planned.
- Decisions have consequences for other people and systems.
- Science and engineering help people solve problems.
- Teamwork, curiosity, and responsible choices matter.

Core moral:

> A great explorer does not only discover new worlds. They think carefully, help others, and make responsible choices to protect the crew.

## 7. Design principles

1. Accuracy before spectacle.
2. Enjoyment through agency, exploration, and feedback.
3. Plain language for children aged 7–12.
4. Two or three core resource meters, not an overloaded dashboard.
5. Meaningful choices with visible consequences.
6. Retry and learn rather than harsh punishment.
7. Browser-first access with no account or installation required for the player.
8. Transparent NASA source attribution.
9. A small working prototype before feature expansion.

## 8. Competition submission honesty

The team is still curating and integrating NASA data. Do not state that all intended sources are already integrated. In submissions, distinguish:

- implemented features;
- planned features;
- NASA datasets actually consulted;
- NASA datasets actually embedded or queried;
- fictional and simplified game mechanics.
