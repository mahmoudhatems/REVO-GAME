# REVO — Mars, Artemis, Energy Research, and Communications Sources

## 1. Perseverance and MEDA at Jezero Crater

Perseverance landed in Jezero Crater on Mars. Its Mars Environmental Dynamics Analyzer (MEDA) measures environmental conditions including wind speed and direction, temperature, humidity, and dust particle amount and size.

Use MEDA as the primary Mars-local environmental reference for REVO's fictional Jezero-area setting.

Potential educational uses:

- why an outpost needs weather sensors;
- why dust and wind matter for outdoor activity;
- how measurements help plan rover or astronaut tasks;
- how scientists monitor the environment over time.

Sources:

- https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mars-2020-perseverance/
- https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mars-2020-perseverance/science-instruments/

## 2. Curiosity and REMS

The Rover Environmental Monitoring Station (REMS) on Curiosity measures Martian atmospheric pressure, temperature, humidity, winds, and ultraviolet radiation and provides daily/seasonal weather reports.

Use REMS as additional historical Mars weather context. Preserve the fact that its measurements come from Curiosity's actual site and mission, not from REVO's fictional base at Jezero.

Source: https://science.nasa.gov/mission/msl-curiosity/science-instruments/

## 3. Opportunity — historical context only

Opportunity's solar-powered mission and historical dust/energy context can be useful when teaching why sunlight and dust matter to solar-powered exploration. REVO's fictional base includes external solar arrays and battery storage; do not transfer another mission's performance values to REVO.

Do not use Opportunity's energy figures as REVO's power-generation values. If used at all, present them as a separate historical science card about a different rover and its actual location at Meridiani Planum—not Jezero.

Sources:

- https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mer-opportunity/
- https://science.nasa.gov/resource/opportunitys-landing-site-meridiani-plain/
- https://science.nasa.gov/resource/on-mars-light-is-energy/

## 4. Artemis / Moon resources

The challenge allows lunar or Martian interpretations and mentions Artemis. Artemis data and reports can be used for comparative content about lunar exploration, crew operations, mission planning, and science.

REVO is a Mars game. Every Artemis-derived item must be explicitly labeled as lunar data and must not be presented as a Martian environmental measurement.

Useful portal:

- https://www.nasa.gov/moonbase-science/
- https://science.data.nasa.gov/
- https://data.nasa.gov/

Use mission-specific reports and archived data products, not only press summaries, if a numerical value will affect gameplay.

## 5. Fission surface power (comparative research only)

NASA has studied fission surface power in coordination with the U.S. Department of Energy for sustained lunar exploration and possible future Mars architectures. NASA describes fission power as an option that can operate without relying on sunlight, including during long periods of darkness.

This supports the plausibility of REVO's fictional outpost power architecture:

- a fission power system is not part of the currently approved REVO MVP;
- an indoor Power Control Room;
- managed distribution to life support, communication, research, and other systems.

This source is retained as background research only. It does not mean a fission reactor is operating at a human Mars base, and the concept is not part of the current approved REVO architecture.

NASA source: https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/fission-system-to-power-exploration-on-the-moons-surface-and-beyond/

## 6. Radiation protection

Use NASA educational references to explain that radiation is a spaceflight hazard and that shielding, habitat design, mission planning, and sheltering are considered in exploration design.

Do not invent a shielding effectiveness percentage or a safe/unsafe dose threshold for the fictional base. If a numerical example is needed, identify the original instrument, environment, unit, time, and limitations.

NASA educational source:

https://science.nasa.gov/resource/real-martians-how-to-protect-astronauts-from-space-radiation-on-mars/

For measurement data, inspect RadLab and OSDR data context in `OSDR_DATA_REFERENCE.md`.

## 7. Mars–Earth communication delay

NASA explains that one-way communication between Earth and Mars varies with planetary distance, commonly ranging from several minutes to roughly twenty-plus minutes. The game can use delayed responses to demonstrate that mission crews cannot rely on instant instructions from Earth.

If the game compresses a long real delay to a few seconds for pacing, label it as a gameplay time compression rather than a literal communications measurement.

NASA reference:

https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/goddard/space-communications-7-things-you-need-to-know/
