# REVO — Learning, Rewards, and Child-Friendly Feedback

## 1. Primary moral

> A great explorer doesn't just discover new worlds. They think carefully, help others, and make responsible choices to protect the crew.

A child aged 7–12 should leave REVO understanding that science helps people solve problems, resources must be used thoughtfully, and a good decision considers the whole team.

## 2. Learning outcomes

The game should help children practice:

- **Think before acting:** consider possible outcomes.
- **Teamwork:** understand that systems and people depend on one another.
- **Responsibility:** use limited resources thoughtfully.
- **Problem-solving:** inspect a problem, compare options, try a solution, and learn from the outcome.
- **Scientific curiosity:** ask why an event happened and connect the explanation to evidence.
- **Resilience:** treat a failed attempt as useful feedback, not as a personal failure.

## 3. Reward philosophy

Reward good reasoning, curiosity, cooperation, resource awareness, and learning—not only a perfect resource score.

Do not create a reward system that encourages a child to ignore crew safety, take reckless risks, or hoard resources without understanding why.

Rewards should be:

- positive and specific;
- understandable without reading long text;
- attainable through different strategies;
- based on observable gameplay actions;
- connected to a learning outcome;
- non-punitive when the player experiments or fails.

## 4. Suggested achievements / mission patches

These are proposed achievement concepts; tune them after playtesting.

| Achievement | Trigger concept | Learning message |
|---|---|---|
| Careful Planner | Makes a resource decision after inspecting the relevant system. | Checking the facts helps you choose. |
| Crew First | Prioritizes a critical life-support or shelter action when appropriate. | People come before optional tasks. |
| Resource Guardian | Avoids unnecessary waste while keeping critical systems supported. | Resources need thoughtful planning. |
| Curious Researcher | Completes a research objective and opens its science card. | Questions help us discover. |
| Problem Solver | Recovers a facility after a fault or setback. | Problems can be understood and fixed step by step. |
| Team Player | Completes a task that benefits more than one facility or crew need. | Working together makes a stronger mission. |
| Adaptable Explorer | Changes strategy after seeing a previous consequence. | Learning helps us make a better next choice. |
| Mission Finisher | Reaches the Day 30 review. | You completed the training mission. |

Do not require every achievement for the best ending.

## 5. Knowledge cards

After selected interactions, show a compact card:

- one short fact;
- one child-friendly explanation;
- one "How this connects to REVO" sentence;
- one source name and link;
- a label such as `NASA RESEARCH`, `HISTORICAL DATA`, or `REVO TRAINING SIMULATION`.

Example structure:

**Why is this important?**  
A Mars rover measures wind and dust to help scientists understand the weather.  
**In REVO:** N.E.I.L. uses a weather update to decide whether outdoor work should continue.  
**Source:** NASA Perseverance — MEDA.

Do not invent numerical measurements for a knowledge card.

## 6. Feedback after choices

Every meaningful decision should show:

1. what changed;
2. what was protected;
3. what was delayed or used;
4. why this happened;
5. what the child can try next time.

Avoid text such as "Wrong!" or "You failed because you are bad at planning." Prefer:

- "Good thinking. You protected the oxygen system, but the lab must wait."
- "The storm makes outdoor work harder. Let's check the shelter before going outside."
- "This choice helped the greenhouse, but it used power needed elsewhere. What could you change next time?"

## 7. End-of-mission recognition

At Day 30, show a mission report rather than only a score.

Include:

- ending classification;
- key decisions;
- crew safety status;
- resource status;
- research discoveries;
- achievements earned;
- one main lesson learned;
- invitation to replay with a different strategy.

A reduced-operations ending can still recognize thoughtful decisions and completed learning objectives.

## 8. Age and accessibility guidance

- Use short sentences and familiar words.
- Introduce only one new science idea at a time.
- Explain `sol` as a Martian day if used.
- Provide simple tooltips for technical terms.
- Use icons plus words; do not rely on color alone.
- Provide readable text size and strong contrast.
- Avoid timers that create unnecessary pressure.
- Make touch targets large enough for mobile play.
- Keep audio optional and provide readable dialogue text.
